# AV Dictionary: The Equipment Terms You'll Actually Hear When Planning an Event

![Black usb cable on a bright yellow background - EventGear PDX](/blog/images/av-dictionary-event-terms-glossary/cover.jpg)

> A plain-language glossary of the AV terms that show up in rental quotes and equipment specs — lumens, throw ratio, dB, XLR, gain, and more — explained for event organizers, not technicians.

You're planning an all-hands, a wedding ceremony, or a parent night, and someone sends over an AV quote or a venue spec sheet. It's full of words like "throw ratio," "gain before feedback," and "lumens," and none of it means anything to you. You're not an AV technician — you shouldn't have to be one to book the right equipment for your event.

## Why Jargon Causes Real Planning Problems

The trouble isn't that these words sound technical. It's that they describe real differences in how equipment performs in your specific room. A projector rated at 2,500 lumens works fine in a dim conference room but washes out completely under gymnasium lights. A wireless mic described as "handheld" behaves differently on stage than the "lavalier" your presenter actually needs clipped to a lapel. If you don't know which term applies to your situation, you can't tell a good recommendation from a wrong one.

## What It Costs You to Guess Wrong

Guessing leads to predictable failures: a screen that's too dim for the back row, a microphone that feeds back the moment someone raises their voice, a PA sized for a living room trying to cover a tent full of 80 guests. These aren't rare mishaps — they're the direct result of matching the wrong spec to the wrong space, and they usually surface mid-event, when there's no time left to fix them.

## A Working AV Dictionary: Terms That Actually Matter

Here's what the common terms mean in practical terms:

- **Lumens** — a projector's brightness. Higher numbers hold up better against windows and room lighting.
- **Throw ratio** — how far the projector needs to sit from the screen to fill it. Determines whether it can go on a back table or needs a short-throw mount.
- **Aspect ratio** — the screen's shape, usually 16:9 (widescreen) or 4:3 (older, boxier). Mismatched slides get cropped or letterboxed.
- **dB SPL** — how loud a sound system can get. A PA rated for a 30-person meeting room won't cover an outdoor ceremony.
- **Gain before feedback** — how much a microphone can be turned up before it squeals. Lower headroom means careful mic placement matters more.
- **Frequency response** — the range of pitches a speaker reproduces, written as a range in Hz. Wider range matters for music, less so for spoken word.
- **XLR / lavalier / handheld** — connector and microphone types. A lavalier clips to clothing for hands-free speaking; a handheld suits an MC moving around.

## Using These Terms When You Call a Rental Company

You don't need to memorize this list to get it right — you need to describe your event honestly and let the terms translate for you. Tell a rental company the room size, whether it's indoors or outdoors, how many people will hear the sound, and whether speakers move around or stay at a podium. A good AV provider converts that into lumens, dB, and mic type on your behalf, and tests the match before the event, not during it. That's the actual value of knowing the vocabulary: not sounding technical, but asking the right questions before you commit to equipment.

## FAQ

**Do I need to know these terms before I get a quote?**
No. Describe your room, guest count, and event type, and a rental company should translate that into the right equipment specs for you. Knowing the terms just helps you evaluate what they recommend.

**What's the difference between a lavalier and a handheld microphone?**
A lavalier clips to clothing and frees up the speaker's hands, which works well for presentations or officiants. A handheld suits someone who moves around or passes the mic to guests, like at a wedding toast.

**Why does throw ratio matter if the projector is bright enough?**
Brightness and distance are separate problems. A projector can be plenty bright but still produce an image too small or too large if it's not positioned at the distance its throw ratio requires.

**Can a PA system that's fine for a conference room handle an outdoor wedding?**
Usually not. Outdoor spaces lack walls to contain sound and often have background noise, so they need higher dB output and often a subwoofer that an indoor-rated system won't provide.

**Why did my microphone feedback at a previous event?**
Feedback usually happens when a mic's gain is pushed past its headroom, often because the speaker is too close to a loudspeaker. Proper placement and a technician monitoring levels during the event prevents it.

## The Bottom Line

AV terminology exists to describe real performance differences, not to make quotes harder to read. You don't need to become fluent in it — you need to describe your event accurately and expect whoever supplies the equipment to match specs to your room, test it beforehand, and have someone on site who understands exactly what every one of these terms means in practice.
