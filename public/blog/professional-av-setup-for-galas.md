# AV for a Fundraising Dinner or Small Gala

![Gala dinner room with projection screen and podium microphone setup](/blog/images/professional-av-setup-for-galas/cover.jpg)

> Sound and projection for a seated dinner of up to about 100 guests — where these events actually go wrong, and what to settle before the room fills.

A fundraising dinner is a harder room than a conference. The audience is seated at round tables facing each other rather than the front, cutlery and conversation set a noise floor that a meeting room never has, and the important moments — the welcome, the story, the ask — happen when people are mid-course and already talking. The equipment involved is modest. The planning is what decides whether the room hears the ask.

## The Room Faces the Wrong Way

Rounds seat people comfortably and leave a third of them with their back to any screen. You can't rotate a dinner mid-service, so the decision belongs to the floor plan: where the speaking position goes, which wall carries the screen, and which tables end up turning their chairs for ten minutes.

A single screen at one end of a long room serves the near half well and the far half poorly. Two smaller screens along the room's length often work better than one large one at the front, and either way the screen has to sit high enough to clear centrepieces and standing guests.

## Sound Has to Beat the Room, Not Fill It

Cutlery, glassware, service, and a hundred conversations are the competition. Volume is not the answer — intelligibility is, and that comes from putting speakers along the length of the room rather than pushing everything from the front.

Microphones follow the program: a handheld or lectern mic for the host, a lavalier for anyone who moves, and a second handheld for a speaker who appears from a table rather than the stage. Fundraising moments in particular tend to hand the microphone around, which is the point at which a single mic starts costing momentum.

## The Moments That Need Planning

**The welcome.** Happens while people are still arriving and seating. Sound needs to be up and tested before the first guest walks in, not during the first course.

**A speech from a table.** Common at these events and routinely unplanned. If the founder, a beneficiary, or a donor speaks from where they're sitting, a microphone has to reach them.

**The video.** A short film mid-dinner is standard, and its audio is the thing most often forgotten: playing it through a laptop is fine in a meeting, inaudible at a dinner. It needs a proper feed into the PA and a level set for a noisy room.

**The auction or the ask.** The one moment where every word matters, and typically the point where the room is loudest.

## FAQ

**How many microphones does a fundraising dinner need?**
Usually at least two: one for the host at the speaking position and one that can reach a speaker at a table. Add a lavalier if anyone presents while moving.

**Where should the screen go in a room with round tables?**
Where the fewest guests have their backs to it, high enough to clear centrepieces. In a long room, two screens along the length often beat one big screen at the end.

**Why does the video never sound right at dinners?**
Because laptop audio can't beat a room full of conversation and cutlery. The film's sound needs to run through the PA with a level set for the room, not through the laptop's speakers.

**Do you provide lighting for galas?**
No — stage and decorative lighting, LED walls and staging aren't part of what we do. We cover sound, projection, microphones and hybrid.

**Can you run the evening as well as set it up?**
Yes, that's included: a technician stays for the whole event and handles the microphone hand-offs, the video playback and the levels.

**How far ahead should we book?**
As soon as the date is fixed. Availability depends on the calendar, and gala season fills up — the equipment list can be refined later.

## The Takeaway

A dinner is a room built for eating, borrowed for a program. Decide the speaking position and the screen with the floor plan, put speech reinforcement along the room rather than at the front, plan for the microphone that has to reach a table, and give the video a real audio path. Get those four right and the ask lands.
