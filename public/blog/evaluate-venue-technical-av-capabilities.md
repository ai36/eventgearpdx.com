# How to Evaluate a Venue's Technical and AV Capabilities Before You Book

![Empty theater with rows of seats and stage - EventGear PDX](/blog/images/evaluate-venue-technical-av-capabilities/cover.jpg)

> A practical checklist for assessing power, acoustics, projection surfaces, and in-house AV restrictions before signing a venue contract.

You've narrowed your event down to a shortlist of venues. The room looks right, the capacity works, the date is open. But before you sign anything, you need to know whether the space can actually support a presentation, a panel discussion, a band, or a hybrid meeting with remote attendees — because a beautiful room with bad acoustics or one power outlet behind a pillar will cost you more than the rental fee ever will.

## Check Power Before You Check Anything Else

Ask how many dedicated circuits are available and where the outlets are physically located relative to where you'll set up screens, speakers, and laptops. A lot of event spaces — converted warehouses, barns, outdoor patios, community halls — were wired for lighting and maybe a coffee maker, not for a PA system, two projectors, and a laptop charging station. Running extension cords across a walkway is a liability issue, not a minor inconvenience. If the venue can't tell you circuit locations and amperage on request, that's a sign nobody there has thought seriously about AV.

## Listen to the Room, Not Just Look at It

High ceilings, hard floors, and bare walls look great in photos and sound terrible with a microphone turned on. Ask whether anyone has run sound in the space before, and if the venue staff can't answer that, that's your answer. Rooms with a lot of glass, concrete, or open volume need more than a single speaker to carry voice clearly to the back row — they often need the system positioned and tuned for that specific space, not just switched on.

## Confirm What's Actually Included vs. What You'll Need to Bring

Many venues advertise "AV equipment" that turns out to be a wall-mounted TV and a single HDMI cable. Ask exactly what's provided: screen size, projector lumens if there's a projector, number of microphone inputs, and whether a house sound system actually reaches every seat. Also ask directly whether the venue requires you to use an in-house AV vendor or charges a fee for bringing in outside equipment — this single question can change your budget significantly, and it's one venues don't always volunteer.

## What Happens When You Skip This Step

The failure mode is predictable: you discover the gaps the morning of the event, when there's no time left to fix them. A keynote speaker's slides project onto a screen too small for the back third of the room. A wireless mic cuts out because the venue's own system wasn't built for a roaming presenter. Remote attendees on a hybrid call can't hear the room because nobody checked whether the wifi could handle a stable upload. None of these are equipment failures — they're the result of not asking the right questions during the walkthrough.

## Bring in What the Venue Can't Provide

A venue doesn't need built-in AV to work for your event — it needs the gaps filled correctly. A walkthrough with an AV rental provider, before you sign the contract, tells you exactly what the space can and can't support: where speakers need to go, whether you need a second projector for sightline issues, how many wireless mic channels the format actually requires. ## FAQ

**What's the single most important thing to check during a venue walkthrough?**
Power — specifically, the number of circuits, their amperage, and where the outlets are relative to your setup. Acoustics and screen placement matter too, but power problems are the hardest to work around on the day of the event.

**Does a venue with an in-house AV system mean I don't need outside equipment?**
Not necessarily. In-house systems are often built for basic background music or a single microphone, not a full presentation with slides, multiple speakers, and audience Q&A. Test it or ask specific questions about capacity before relying on it.

**Should I ask about outside-vendor fees before booking?**
Yes, always, and get it in writing. Some venues restrict you to a preferred AV vendor or charge a fee if you bring in your own equipment — this can affect your total budget as much as the venue rental itself.

**How do I know if a venue's wifi can support a hybrid or streamed event?**
Ask for the upload speed, not just the download speed, and ask if it's shared with other tenants or events happening simultaneously. A streaming setup needs consistent upload bandwidth, which is where most venue wifi actually falls short.

**Can an outdoor or non-traditional venue (barn, backyard, tent) work for a presentation-heavy event?**
Yes, with the right equipment matched to the space — these venues just need power, sound, and screen solutions planned in advance rather than assumed to exist.

## The Bottom Line

Evaluating a venue's technical capabilities isn't about finding a perfect room — few exist. It's about identifying the gaps early enough to fill them deliberately: confirm power locations, test the acoustics honestly, get a precise list of what's actually included, and ask about outside-vendor restrictions before you sign.
