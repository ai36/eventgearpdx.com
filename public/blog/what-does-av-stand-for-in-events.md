# What "AV" Means — and the Words That Come With It

![Event planner reviewing AV setup with projector screen and speaker system in a Portland conference room](/blog/images/what-does-av-stand-for-in-events/cover.jpg)

> AV, AV room, AV needs, the AV input on a projector: one abbreviation, several meanings depending on who says it.

AV stands for audiovisual — the sound and picture side of an event. That's the whole of the abbreviation, but almost none of the confusion, because the same two letters show up in a venue contract, a quote, a job title, and a menu on the back of a projector, meaning something different each time. Here's each use, briefly.

## AV, the Category

In an event context, AV covers everything that makes a presenter audible and their content visible: projectors and screens or displays, PA speakers, microphones, the cabling and adapters that join them, and — increasingly — the cameras and audio routing that let remote attendees take part.

It doesn't normally cover stage lighting, staging, or entertainment services, even though a single quote sometimes lists them together. Those are separate trades. We don't provide them.

## "AV Room"

A venue that advertises an AV room means a room with some equipment installed — typically a ceiling projector or a wall display, sometimes speakers, occasionally a microphone. It is not a promise about capability. The useful follow-up questions are how many microphone channels exist, whether the house speakers can carry speech rather than background music, and what inputs are available where the presenter will stand. Our guide on [verifying what a venue covers](/blog/conference-center-audio-visual-equipment) goes through this in detail.

## "AV Needs"

This is what a supplier means by "tell us your AV needs": not a shopping list, but the facts that produce one — room size and daylight, headcount, who speaks and from where, and whether anyone joins remotely. The method is in [working out what AV your meeting needs](/blog/how-to-estimate-av-equipment-needs-corporate-events).

## "AV Input" on a Projector or TV

Here the abbreviation means something narrower and older: the composite video input, usually a yellow RCA socket alongside red and white audio jacks. It's a legacy standard from the VHS and DVD era, and its picture quality is well below HDMI.

If you're connecting a modern laptop, you want HDMI or USB-C, not the AV input. Seeing a black screen after switching to AV usually means the input is simply the wrong one for the source you've plugged in.

## "AV Technician" and "AV Setup"

An AV technician is the person who places, connects, tests, and runs the equipment during an event. "AV setup" is the work itself — and the word hides a lot, because it can mean anything from dropping equipment at a loading dock to running the room for the duration. That distinction is the single most important thing to check in a quote, and it's covered in [what "AV set up" actually includes](/blog/av-set-up-meaning).

## FAQ

**What does AV stand for?**
Audiovisual — the audio and video side of an event: sound, microphones, projection and displays, and the equipment that connects them.

**Is an "AV room" ready to present in?**
Not necessarily. It means equipment is installed, not that it suits your audience size or program. Check the microphone count, whether the speakers carry speech, and what inputs are at the lectern.

**What is the AV input on a projector?**
The composite video socket — usually yellow, often beside red and white audio jacks. It's a legacy connection with much lower quality than HDMI, and it's not what a modern laptop should use.

**Does AV include lighting and staging?**
Usually not. Stage lighting, staging, and DJ services are separate from audio and video, and we don't provide them — projectors, screens, PA, microphones, and hybrid support are our scope.

**What are "AV needs" in a quote request?**
The facts that determine equipment: room and daylight, headcount, distance to the back row, who speaks, and whether anyone attends remotely.

## The Takeaway

AV is a short word for a long list, and the list changes with who is using it. Whenever the abbreviation appears in a contract or a quote, the productive move is to ask what specifically is included — equipment, people, or both.
