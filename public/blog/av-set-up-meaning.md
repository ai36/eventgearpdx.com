# AV Set Up: What the Term Actually Covers

![AV technician setting up speakers and a projector screen in a Portland event venue](/blog/images/av-set-up-meaning/cover.jpg)

> "AV set up" means different things to a venue, a rental company, and the person organizing an event. Here's what each of them usually means by it.

If you've been quoted for an "AV set up," the word hides most of the detail. To one supplier it means equipment arriving in cases. To another it means equipment arriving, being assembled, tested, and run by someone who stays. To a venue it can mean a projector already bolted to the ceiling and a microphone in a cupboard. The differences are worth knowing before you compare two quotes that look similar.

## The Equipment Side

At minimum, "AV" covers the audio and video a room needs: something to show slides (a projector and screen, or a display), something to make a presenter audible (a PA and microphones), and the cabling, stands and adapters that connect them. For events with remote attendees it also covers capturing the room's audio and video into a video call.

## The Work Side

The word "set up" is doing a lot of work in that phrase. It can include any of: delivery to the room, assembly and placement, cable runs and taping, connecting a presenter's laptop, testing the whole chain with real content, running the equipment during the event, and taking it all out afterwards.

This is where quotes diverge. Two prices for "AV set up" can mean "you'll get boxes at the loading dock" and "someone will be in the room the entire time." Ask specifically: who assembles it, who tests it, who is present during the event, and who removes it.

## What It Usually Doesn't Include

Permanent installation is a different trade — fixing displays or speakers into a room for good is an installation project, not an event set up. Stage lighting, staging, and entertainment services like a DJ are also separate from AV in most quotes, even when the same word appears on the invoice.

## FAQ

**Does "AV set up" include someone running the event?**
Not always — and that's the most important thing to check in a quote. Some suppliers deliver equipment only. Every booking with us includes a technician who stays for the whole event.

**Is AV set up the same as AV installation?**
No. Installation means permanently fitting equipment into a room. A set up is temporary: it arrives for your event and leaves afterwards.

**What equipment does a basic AV set up include?**
Usually a screen and projector, a PA with at least one microphone, and the cabling and adapters to connect a presenter's laptop. What your room actually needs depends on the size, the daylight, and who is speaking.

**Does AV set up cover lighting and staging?**
Typically not, and we don't provide them. Stage lighting, staging, LED walls, and DJ services are separate from audio and video equipment even when a single quote lists them together.

**Who connects the presenter's laptop?**
In a full-service set up, the technician does, and tests it before the room fills. If a quote doesn't say who does this, it's worth asking — the laptop connection is the most common point of failure at a presentation.

## The Takeaway

"AV set up" is a phrase that needs unpacking every time it appears in a quote. The equipment list matters less than who does the work: who places it, who tests it with the real presentation, who stays in the room, and who takes it away. Ask that, and two comparable-looking quotes usually stop looking comparable.
