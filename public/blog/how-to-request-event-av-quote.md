# How to Request an Event AV Quote That Actually Gets You Accurate Pricing

![a laptop computer sitting on top of a desk - EventGear PDX](/blog/images/how-to-request-event-av-quote/cover.jpg)

> What to include when requesting an AV equipment quote for your event, and why vague requests lead to wrong gear, wrong pricing, or both.

You've got a date, a venue, and a headcount. Now someone has to figure out the sound and video for the event, and that job has landed on you. Maybe it's a 150-person conference, a hotel ballroom banquet, or a school auditorium program. You know you need a quote from an AV rental company, but you're not sure what information actually makes that quote useful versus a rough guess you'll have to redo twice.

## A Vague Request Gets You a Vague Quote

If you email "I need a projector and some microphones for an event," you'll get back a price range, not a real number. The rental company doesn't know if you need a 3,000-lumen projector for a dim conference room or a 7,000-lumen unit to fight ambient light in a ballroom with windows. They don't know if "some microphones" means two handhelds for a Q&A or six lavaliers for a panel. Every missing detail turns into a follow-up email, and every follow-up email pushes your booking closer to your event date.

## Room Size and Layout Change the Equipment List

Speaker count and room dimensions aren't just details, they determine what actually works. A PA system sized for 50 people in a conference room will sound thin and possibly distorted in a 300-person hall. A screen sized for a small meeting room will be unreadable from the back row of a banquet hall. Quotes based on incomplete room information often have to be revised once someone actually sees the space, and that revision can mean a different price and different gear than what you budgeted for.

## The Cost of Getting a Wrong Quote Twice

When a quote has to be corrected close to the event date, you're now choosing between accepting equipment that doesn't quite fit the room or scrambling for a last-minute substitution, sometimes at a rush rate. A nonprofit gala that discovers three days out that its microphone count was wrong doesn't have room to fix that calmly. Getting the specifics right at the quote stage is what keeps your event budget and your event day both intact.

## What to Include When You Request a Quote

Give the rental company your event date, venue name and room, expected attendance, and how the space will be used, presentation, panel, live music, or a mix. List what you already know you need (projector and screen, wireless mics, PA speakers) and flag what you're unsure about so they can recommend a fit. Mention whether you need delivery, on-site setup, or just equipment pickup. A quote built on this level of detail usually needs no revision, and it lets the company confirm same-day or next-day availability if your event is coming up fast.

## Local Delivery Removes a Layer of Guesswork

For events in the Portland metro area, requesting a quote that includes delivery and on-site setup means one less thing you're coordinating yourself. Equipment gets tested before it leaves the warehouse, and a technician can confirm it's working correctly in the actual room, not just on paper.

## FAQ

**How far in advance should I request an AV quote?**
Two to three weeks out gives the most flexibility for equipment selection and delivery scheduling, but same-day and next-day availability exists for events that come together fast.

**Does the quote include setup, or just the equipment rental?**
That depends on what you ask for. Say clearly whether you want delivery and on-site setup included or if you're picking up equipment yourself, since the price and the crew involved differ.

**What if I'm not sure exactly what equipment I need?**
Describe the room, headcount, and how it'll be used. A rental company can recommend the right projector lumens, speaker count, or microphone type based on that instead of you guessing at model numbers.

## The Bottom Line

An accurate AV quote starts with specifics: venue, room size, attendance, and how the space will be used. Skip the guesswork and you get a price and equipment list that holds up through your event date instead of one that needs fixing at the last minute. If you're planning an event in the Portland metro area, request a quote with those details ready and you'll get a firm answer, not a placeholder.
