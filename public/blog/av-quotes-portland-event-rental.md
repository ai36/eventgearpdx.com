# How to Request an AV Quote and Compare What Comes Back

![Event planner reviewing AV rental quote documents at a desk with a laptop](/blog/images/av-quotes-portland-event-rental/cover.jpg)

> What to send when you ask for an AV quote, and how to read two proposals that look similar but aren't.

Most AV quotes are answers to an incomplete question. "How much for a projector and a microphone on the 14th?" produces a number that can't survive contact with the actual room, and the revision arrives at the worst moment — usually the week of the event. A good quote request takes five minutes to write and removes almost all of that risk.

## What to Send

Six things are enough for a realistic number without a site visit:

- **Venue and room.** The name and room, if it has one. Suppliers who work in the area often know the space.
- **Date and schedule.** Start and end, plus when the room is free for setup and when it must be cleared.
- **Headcount.** And the seating shape if you know it — rows, rounds, U-shape.
- **Who speaks.** One presenter, a panel, a moderator, questions from the floor. This sets the microphone count.
- **Remote attendees.** Whether anyone joins by video call, and whether they need to be heard as well as hear.
- **The room's own conditions.** Daylight and whether it can be blocked, ceiling height, and where the power outlets are if you know.

If the venue has an exclusive AV contractor or charges a fee for outside AV, say so upfront. It changes what anyone can legitimately offer you.

## How to Compare What Comes Back

Two quotes with the same equipment list can describe completely different services. Read for these:

**Who is in the room during the event.** The most expensive difference between quotes is usually a person. Delivery-only, setup-then-leave, and a technician who stays are three different products.

**What "setup" covers.** Placement only, or placement plus testing with your presenter's laptop and slides? The second is what prevents the classic twenty-minute delay at the start.

**Microphone channels, specifically.** Not "wireless microphone" but how many, and of what kind. A panel quoted with one handheld is a quote for a different event than yours.

**Strike and removal.** When equipment comes out, and whether that's inside the venue's access window.

**What's excluded.** Lighting, staging, LED walls, and DJ services are separate trades. A quote that silently includes or excludes them is worth a question.

## Timing

Ask early rather than precisely. Availability depends on the schedule, so the date being fixed matters more than the equipment list being final — details can be adjusted later, a date that's already booked can't. We don't promise a turnaround time; what we can do is tell you quickly whether a date is workable.

## FAQ

**What's the minimum you need to quote?**
Venue and room, date and schedule, headcount, who speaks, whether anyone is remote, and the room's daylight and access. That's usually enough without visiting the space.

**Why do two AV quotes differ so much for the same gear?**
Usually labour. One may be equipment dropped off, another may include setup, testing, and a technician present for the event. Compare the service, not the item list.

**Should I tell suppliers my budget?**
It helps. With a figure to work against, a supplier can tell you what fits and what to drop rather than guessing at a specification you'll reject.

**Is a quote binding if the room turns out to be different?**
A quote is only as good as the room description it was built on. If the layout, headcount, or access changes materially, expect it to be revisited — which is why the upfront detail matters.

**Can I get a quote for equipment only?**
Not from us. Setup, testing, and a technician for the event are part of every booking, so there isn't an equipment-only rate to quote.

**How far ahead should I ask?**
As soon as the date is set. Sizing can be refined later; availability is the part that runs out.

## The Takeaway

A quote request is a specification. Send the room, the schedule, the headcount, who speaks and who dials in, and what comes back will be comparable between suppliers and stable as the date approaches. Send "a projector and a mic," and you'll get a number that changes.
