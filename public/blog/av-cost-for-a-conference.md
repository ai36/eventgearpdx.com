# How Much Does AV Cost for a Conference?

![a conference room with a projector screen and chairs - EventGear PDX](/blog/images/av-cost-for-a-conference/cover.jpg)

> A breakdown of what drives AV pricing for a conference — room size, equipment list, staffing, and how to avoid paying for gear you don't need.

You're planning a conference — maybe fifty people in a hotel ballroom, maybe eighty in a rented hall with a keynote speaker and a few breakout sessions. The venue has tables, chairs, maybe a podium. It does not have a sound system that reaches the back row, a projector sized for the room, or a microphone that won't cut out mid-sentence. Now you need a number for the budget line that says "AV," and most quotes you find online are either vague ranges or written for stadium-scale productions that have nothing to do with your event.

## Why AV Quotes Vary So Much

A single wireless mic and a small speaker for a 30-person meeting might run a few hundred dollars. A full setup for 100 people — projector and screen, a PA system with multiple speakers, two or three wireless mics, and a technician on site for the day — can run into four figures. The spread isn't padding; it reflects genuinely different equipment lists. Room size changes speaker count and placement. Number of presenters changes how many mics you need and whether you need a mixer to switch between them cleanly.

## What Actually Drives the Price

Three factors move the number more than anything else: attendee count (it determines sound coverage and screen size), number of presenters or panelists (each one needs a working mic, and lavalier kits cost more than a single handheld), and whether you need streaming support for remote attendees. A half-day single-room conference prices very differently than a two-track event with breakout rooms running simultaneously, each needing its own projector and sound.

## The Hidden Cost of Underbudgeting

Organizers who go the cheapest possible route — a borrowed projector, a single consumer speaker, no backup mic — often find out the gap during the event itself, when the keynote speaker's voice doesn't reach the back third of the room or the projector washes out under the venue's lighting. At that point there's no fix available; the budget line that looked like a savings becomes a conference that half the room couldn't hear.

## What a Rental Quote Should Include

A proper AV quote for a conference covers the equipment, delivery, on-site setup and testing before doors open, and a technician staying through the event to handle mic swaps, volume adjustments, and any mid-event troubleshooting. That staffing is what separates a rental quote from a bare equipment list — a dead battery or a loose cable during a panel discussion gets fixed in seconds by someone already in the room, instead of derailing the session.

## Getting an Accurate Number

The fastest way to get a real quote instead of a guess is to describe the room: approximate square footage, attendee count, number of presenters, and whether any session needs to stream to remote viewers. That's enough to size a sound system correctly and avoid paying for speaker coverage the room doesn't need — or worse, under-ordering for a room that does.

## FAQ

**What's a reasonable starting budget for a 50-person conference?**
For a single room with one or two presenters, a basic package — small PA system, wireless mic, and projector/screen — typically starts in the low hundreds and scales up with mic count and streaming needs. Exact pricing depends on the room and how long you need the equipment.

**Does the quote include a technician, or just the equipment?**
A full-service quote includes delivery, setup, testing before the event starts, and a technician on site for the duration — not just a box of gear dropped at the door.

**Do I need a different setup for breakout sessions?**
Yes. Each simultaneous breakout room needs its own projector and sound coverage sized to that room, which is a separate line item from the main session's AV.

**What if the venue already has a screen but no sound system?**
That's common in hotel ballrooms and rented halls. You'd only need to fill the gap — a PA system and microphones — rather than paying for a full AV package, which brings the cost down.

**Should I ask the venue about outside-vendor fees before booking equipment?**
Yes — some venues have an exclusive in-house AV provider or charge a fee for bringing in outside equipment. Check this before finalizing any rental.

**How far in advance should I book AV for a conference?**
As early as your date is confirmed. Equipment availability depends on the schedule, and conference season dates fill up well ahead of the event.

## The Takeaway

AV cost for a conference isn't one number — it's the sum of room size, presenter count, and whether remote attendees are part of the picture. The real risk isn't overspending, it's underbudgeting on sound coverage or presenter mics and finding out during the keynote. Describe your room and session plan accurately, get a quote built around the actual event, and book it as early as your date is set.
